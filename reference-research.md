# Reference research — HCAHPS overall star ratings 2017 vs 2024

**Manuscript title (working, per analyst):** *Determinants of Hospital HCAHPS Star Ratings and Changes Over Time, 2017-2024*
**Last updated:** 2026-05-21
**Target journal:** American Journal of Public Health (primary, as of 2026-05-16 journal switch); Health Affairs, JAMA Health Forum, and Health Affairs Scholar as alternates

---

## Gap statement after literature review

Hospital-level analyses of HCAHPS exist in two main forms: (a) cross-sectional determinant studies linking ratings to hospital and community characteristics, and (b) longitudinal description of national trends, especially the COVID-era decline. **No identified study models hospital-level *change* in overall star ratings between 2017 and 2024 with joint hospital, operational, and community-level covariates.** The closest prior work — Herrin et al. (2018) on community factors and Elliott/Beckett et al. (2023, 2025) on COVID-era trends — does not decompose levels-vs-change at the hospital level.

## Journal recommendation: American Journal of Public Health (primary)

**Why AJPH (per the 2026-05-16 switch from Health Affairs)**
- Audience tilts more public-health-academic than Health Affairs' payer/policy mix; voice can be slightly more methodologically explicit (R² contrast, coefficient signs) without losing the policy audience.
- Word budget is more generous (3,500 main text; 180-word structured abstract) than Health Affairs, giving room for a self-contained Methods section rather than an appendix-heavy treatment.
- Exhibit budget typically allows ~4–6 tables and figures combined; the "one primary map + Tables 1 and 2 in main text" plan fits comfortably.
- The paired policy reading (incentive-program problem on the levels side; hospital agency on the change side) lands cleanly at AJPH — both halves are within the journal's remit.

**Backups, in order of fit**
1. **Health Affairs** — original primary target; strongest policy reach; reconsider if AJPH declines and the framing needs to lead with payer/policy stakes rather than public-health implications.
2. **JAMA Health Forum** — Elliott 2023 published here. Closest fit if reviewers see the work as extending the COVID-trend literature. Higher impact factor; faster turnaround.
3. **Health Affairs Scholar** — Beckett 2025 (CAH) published here. Open-access sister to Health Affairs; shorter cycle; same audience.
4. **Medical Care** — Beckett/Lehrman trends and Fitzpatrick Rosenbaum nursing both here. Best fit if methods become more central than policy.
5. **Health Services Research** — broader empirical scope, somewhat less policy reach.
