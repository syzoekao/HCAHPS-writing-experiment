# Grading rubric

The grader reads this file on every run. Edit the definitions to refine your sense of each tier as you use the system. The agents follow these definitions, so changing this file changes future grading.

## `[core]`

Essential to the central argument. The reader cannot follow the logic without this bullet. Removing it breaks the chain.

Examples:
- The primary research question or aim
- The key finding that answers the question
- The methodological choice that determines whether a reader trusts the result
- A bridge bullet whose absence would break a paragraph-to-paragraph transition

## `[support]`

Adds depth, context, or nuance. The argument survives without it, but the reader's understanding is poorer.

Examples:
- Background context that situates the work
- Secondary findings
- Methodological detail that informs interpretation but does not determine it

## `[optional]`

Enrichment. Cuttable under word pressure with no harm to the central argument.

Examples:
- Tangential context
- Speculative discussion points
- Caveats that do not change practical interpretation

## `[?]` (placeholder, not a grade)

The bullet contains only `[TBD]` or otherwise unresolved content. Grade after resolving.

## Notes on use

- Grade based on substance, not bullet length. A short `[TBD]` placeholder for a key finding is `[core]`.
- Bridge bullets that carry transition flow are at least `[support]`, never `[optional]`.
- `[core]` should be a minority of bullets in most outlines. If more than half your bullets grade `[core]`, the rubric or the outline needs scrutiny.
